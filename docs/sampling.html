01

The rejection loop

A draw at or above the bound is thrown away whole, and the next one is taken.

below(bound) reads the bound as a big-endian number and draws as many bytes as bound - 1 needs. The unused high bits of the first byte are cleared, and the draw is turned into a number. If that number is below the bound it is the answer; if it is not, the whole draw is discarded and another takes its place.

What comes back is as wide as the bound, so a bound of b"\x03\xe8" answers with two bytes, not an Int. A bound that is a power of two never pays for the loop: the mask already fits.

Below, the pool is filled once from the platform's own source, and every call starts at its front — so pressing twice on the same bound shows the same draws twice.

02

A scalar

Sixty-six bytes for a P-521 key, with seven bits shifted out of every draw.

scalar(order) returns a value in [1, order - 1], as wide as the order. The order of P-521 is 521 bits, which is 66 bytes with seven bits to spare: the draw is shifted right by seven rather than masked, so a byte at the bottom becomes a value in the low place, and the answer keeps the order's width.

The two red cells below are the only place a value can reach: the first byte's top seven bits are clear, and the last byte holds what was shifted out.

press draw
One call to scalar over a fresh replay. A draw of zeros, or one at or above the order, is discarded and taken again — the panel shows the answer, not the attempts.
